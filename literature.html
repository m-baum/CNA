Literature

Methodological Applied Conceptual Protocol Book chapters PhD theses

The methodological literature on CNA and the applied literature using CNA is rapidly growing. This page provides a mere snapshot. For a full list of CNA resources visit the Zotero CNA Group library.

Methodological

The details of the state of the art of the CNA method, as implemented in the cna R package, are described in:

  • Baumgartner, M. and Ambühl, M. (2025), cna: An R Package for Configurational Causal Inference and Modeling, R package vignette, The Comprehensive R Archive Network.
  • Baumgartner, M. and Ambühl, M. (2020), Causal Modeling with Multi-Value and Fuzzy-Set Coincidence Analysis, Political Science Research and Methods 8, 526-542, doi: 10.1017/psrm.2018.45. Replication material at 10.7910/DVN/YIEAF1.

Short and informal introductions to configurational causal modeling are provided in the following papers:

  • von Thiele Schwarz, U., Sørensen, O. H., Tafvelin, S., and Roczniewska, M. (2024), Complexity Embraced: A New Perspective on the Evaluation of Organisational Interventions, Work & Stress, 1-7, doi: 10.1080/02678373.2024.2349000
  • Whitaker, R.G., Sperber, N., Baumgartner, M., et al. (2020), Coincidence Analysis: A New Method for Causal Inference in Implementation Science, Implementation Science 15, 108, doi: 10.1186/s13012-020-01070-3
  • Distel, B., Plattfaut, R., and Baumgartner, M. (2026), Coincidence Analysis: An Alternative Method for Studying Causal Complexity in IS Research, Communications of the Association for Information Systems 59.

This paper introduces CNA robustness scoring, as implemented in the frscore R package:

  • Parkkinen, V.P. and Baumgartner, M. (2023), Robustness and Model Selection in Configurational Causal Modeling, Sociological Methods & Research 52(1), 176-208, doi: 10.1177/0049124120986200

This paper discusses a protocol for finding optimally obtainable consistency and coverage scores for a given data set. (The algorithms introduced in this paper are implemented in the cnaOpt R package):

  • Baumgartner, M. and Ambühl, M. (2021), Optimizing Consistency and Coverage in Configurational Causal Modeling, Sociological Methods & Research, doi: 10.1177/0049124121995554

These papers uncover shortcomings of consistency and coverage and introduce two evaluation measures:

  • De Souter, L. (2024), Evaluating Boolean Relationships in Configurational Comparative Methods, Journal of Causal Inference 12(1), doi: 10.1515/jci-2023-0014
  • De Souter, L. and Baumgartner, M. (2025), New Sufficiency and Necessity Measures for Model Building with Coincidence Analysis, Zenodo, doi: 10.5281/zenodo.13619580 (preprint).

This paper investigates the impact of data imbalances (skewness) on CNA’s performance:

  • Swiatczak, M. and Baumgartner, M. (2024), Data Imbalances in Coincidence Analysis: A Simulation Study, Sociological Methods & Research, doi: 10.1177/00491241241227039

These papers compare CNA and QCA:

  • Swiatczak, M. (2022), Different Algorithms, Different Models, Quality & Quantity 56, 1913-1937
  • Haesebrouck, T. and Thomann, E. (2022), Introduction: Causation, Inferences, and Solution Types in Configurational Comparative Methods, Quality & Quantity 56, 1867-1888

This paper compares CNA and Logic Regression:

  • Baumgartner, M. and Falk, C. (2023), Configurational Causal Modeling and Logic Regression, Multivariate Behavioral Research 58(2), 292-310

Older versions of the CNA method are introduced/discussed here:

  • Baumgartner, M. and Thiem, A. (2015), Identifying Complex Causal Dependencies in Configurational Data with Coincidence Analysis, The R Journal 7, 176-184
  • Baumgartner, M. (2013), Detecting Causal Chains in Small-N Data, Field Methods 25, 3-24
  • Baumgartner, M. (2009), Uncovering Deterministic Causal Structures: A Boolean Approach, Synthese 170, 71-96
  • Baumgartner, M. (2009), Inferring Causal Complexity, Sociological Methods & Research 38, 71-101
